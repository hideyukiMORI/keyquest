# Product Direction

## Core Promise

KeyQuest is a terminal typing adventure that helps players feel real improvement
through 10 minutes of daily English typing practice.

The ideal first journey lasts 90 days. That is long enough for accuracy, rhythm,
symbols, and confidence to visibly improve, but short enough to feel like a
complete RPG season with an ending.

## Player Fantasy

The player is a novice adventurer restoring the lost "Keys of Words" in a classic
fantasy kingdom. The story can be familiar and direct: villages, forests, ruins,
dragons, ancient magic, and a final confrontation with the Lord of Silence.

The originality should come from the training design, not from an overly complex
plot. Players should always understand where they are, why they are practicing,
and what tomorrow's quest will unlock.

## Practice Loop

Daily target: 10 minutes.

1. Start the daily quest.
2. Warm up with familiar prompts.
3. Face one focused training theme.
4. Review weak keys from recent mistakes.
5. Resolve the quest with score, rewards, achievements, and story progress.

During typing, the screen should stay calm. Rewards, story, inventory, and
progression should mostly appear after a typing segment so the game never fights
the practice.

## 90-Day Arc

- Days 1-7: onboarding, home row, basic rhythm, first boss.
- Days 8-30: common words, simple punctuation, first kingdom restored.
- Days 31-60: programmer symbols, mixed prompts, weak-key review, second kingdom.
- Days 61-89: longer passages, accuracy under pressure, advanced symbol runs.
- Day 90: final quest and ending.

After the ending, the game should support optional post-game practice: higher
difficulty, streak goals, completion achievements, and new-game-plus style
progression.

## Success Criteria

- Players can explain how they improved, not only that they gained levels.
- Accuracy improves before speed is rewarded heavily.
- Missing a day hurts less than quitting. Returning should be celebrated.
- The game is satisfying at 10 minutes but still tempting when players want more.
- All typing prompts are English; UI messages are localized.
